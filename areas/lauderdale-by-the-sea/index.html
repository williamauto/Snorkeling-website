Lauderdale-by-the-Sea

A compact beach town built around a shore-entry reef just 100 yards out, no boat needed for most of it.

Nearly everything here is walk-in from the beach.Free (public beach access)6 named spots

Best time to go

Go early morning before the wind picks up and the beach crowds arrive; an outgoing tide keeps the water clearest over the reef line.

Getting there

Public beach access and metered parking line A1A between Commercial Boulevard and El Prado Park; the Datura Avenue lot puts you closest to the wreck trail.

Shipwreck Snorkel Trail

A shallow, shore-entry trail off Datura Avenue laid with replica 19th-century wreck artifacts: concrete cannons, an anchor, and ballast stones, about a 5-minute swim out.

Depth
8–10 ft
Getting there
Walk-in from Datura Avenue beach; no boat or tour needed.
Fee
Free
Marine life
arrow crabs, scorpionfish, spiny lobster, moray eels, nurse sharks, sea turtles, rays
Tip: Enter at the Datura Avenue beach access and swim straight out; the trail markers are easiest to spot on a calm, sunny morning.

First Reef Line

The natural reef line running parallel to the beach, with coral heads rising 3-4 feet and creviced structures worth exploring.

Depth
12–20 ft
Getting there
Walk-in, roughly 100 yards offshore.
Fee
Free
Marine life
brain coral, star coral, grunts, snappers, blennies, moray eels
Tip: Follow the reef line north or south from the rental shop near the beach access; look for the taller coral heads where the current runs slightly stronger.

The Potholes

Natural depressions between the wreck trail and the second reef that act as a nursery habitat, good for watching juvenile fish.

Depth
8–15 ft
Getting there
Walk-in, south of Anglin's Pier.
Fee
Free
Marine life
juvenile fish, parrotfish, tarpon, baitfish schools
Tip: A good stop for families since the depressions are shallow and sheltered; watch for baitfish schools flashing in the sun.

Anglin's Ledge

A low ledge formation near the pier with more current than the trail spots; comfortable swimmers only.

Depth
10–14 ft
Getting there
Walk-in near Anglin's Pier.
Fee
Free
Marine life
goatfish, surgeonfish, reef sharks, sea turtles
Tip: Current picks up close to the pier pilings; stronger swimmers only, and check conditions before heading in on a windy day.

The Nursery (El Prado Park)

A quieter shore entry point than Datura Avenue, named for the young fish that shelter here.

Depth
8–15 ft
Getting there
Walk-in from El Prado Park beach access.
Fee
Free
Marine life

Tip: A calmer, less crowded entry than Datura Avenue; a good pick if the main beach access is busy.

Biorock Project

A mineral-accretion reef restoration structure, where a low-voltage current grows coral onto a steel frame, open to snorkelers as a living conservation site.

Depth
10–14 ft
Getting there
Walk-in, part of the LBTS reef line.
Fee
Free
Marine life
juvenile coral colonies, reef fish
Tip: Look, but do not touch the frame; it is a working coral restoration project, not just a dive attraction.
