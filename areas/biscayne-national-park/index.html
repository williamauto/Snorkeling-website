Biscayne National Park

About 95 percent of the park is underwater, so the only way to see the reef is by boat, tour, or the park concessionaire.

Boat or tour required; no shore reef access.Free park entry; boat/tour required for water access5 named spots

Best time to go

Book the earliest concessionaire trip of the day for the calmest water and best visibility.

Getting there

The Dante Fascell Visitor Center in Homestead runs the park's boat and snorkel tours; a private boat needs its own launch and permit.

Boca Chita Key

The park's most visited island, with a historic 1930s lighthouse and calm shore snorkeling along the beach.

Depth
3–10 ft
Getting there
Boat access to the island (private boat or park tour), then shore snorkeling from the beach.
Fee
Park entrance fee applies; boat docking fee for private boats
Marine life
tropical reef fish, sea turtles, rays
Tip: Reach it by private boat or the park's guided eco-adventure tours from the Dante Fascell Visitor Center.
Source: Boca Chita Key - Biscayne National Park (NPS)

Elliott Key

The largest island in the park, with a campground, hiking trail, and calm bay-side swimming and snorkeling.

Depth
3–12 ft
Getting there
Boat access to the island, then swimming and snorkeling from the shore.
Fee
Park entrance fee applies; camping/docking fees for overnight
Marine life
tropical reef fish, mangrove snapper, rays
Tip: The bay side is calmest; the Atlantic side has more current.
Source: Places To Go - Biscayne National Park (NPS)

Biscayne National Park Reef Snorkel

Part of the Great Florida Reef, reachable through the park concessionaire's boat tours or a private boat; about 95 percent of the park is underwater.

Depth
10–25 ft
Getting there
Free park entry, but reef access is by boat, tour, or your own vessel; there is no shore reef.
Fee
Free park entry; boat/tour required for water access
Marine life
sea turtles, puffer fish, angelfish, moray eels, blue tangs, assorted coral
Tip: Book the concessionaire tour well ahead in winter high season, when weekend trips sell out.

Boat access

Reachable by private boat or a charter that will run you out and back.

Reef
Boat accessBeginner-friendly

Jones Lagoon

A massive, shallow reef system near the Jones Family Historic District, with plenty of space to explore away from other visitors.

Depth
5–15 ft
Getting there
Boat access; rangers recommend this massive reef for snorkeling away from the crowds.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
rays, upside-down jellyfish, tropical reef fish, wading birds
Tip: Navigate with the mooring buoys and give the bird rookery plenty of space.
Source: Everything you should know about Biscayne National Park - National Geographic
Reef
Boat accessIntermediate

Fowey Rocks

A reef system around the 1878 Fowey Rocks Lighthouse, with mooring buoys and abundant marine life.

Depth
10–25 ft
Getting there
Boat access to the reef around the historic Fowey Rocks Lighthouse.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
elkhorn coral, grouper, snapper, sea turtles
Tip: Currents can pick up here; go with a guide or experienced boat operator.
Source: Biscayne National Park (NPS)
