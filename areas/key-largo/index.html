Key Largo

John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park and the offshore reefs that made Key Largo the dive capital of the Keys.

One shore option inside the state park; the famous offshore reefs are boat or tour access.John Pennekamp: $8/vehicle8 named spots

Best time to go

Morning charters usually beat the afternoon wind chop; Cannon Beach inside the park is calm most days.

Getting there

John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park has its own entrance and parking on Route 1; boat trips to Molasses Reef and Christ of the Abyss leave from marinas along the same stretch.

Cannon Beach, John Pennekamp State Park

The park's shore-entry snorkel area, named for a replica Spanish shipwreck cannon display about 100 feet offshore. It's the easiest, most beginner-friendly entry point in Key Largo.

Depth
3–10 ft
Getting there
Walk-in from the park's Cannon Beach or Far Beach, inside the park gate.
Fee
$8/vehicle park entrance
Marine life
tropical reef fish
Tip: Rent gear at the park's own concession stand; no need to bring your own if you are just getting started.

Tour required

Booked through a licensed operator; usually not open to private boats.

Christ of the Abyss (Dry Rocks)

A 9-foot bronze statue of Christ on Dry Rocks reef, one of the most photographed snorkel sites in the Keys.

Depth
25–30 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required; it's a standard stop for Key Largo snorkel and dive charters.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
grouper, grunts, angelfish, nurse sharks, moray eels
Tip: Book with a morning departure for the calmest water and the clearest shot of the statue.

Grecian Rocks

A protected, horseshoe-shaped reef about a mile south of Dry Rocks with a shallow crest that makes it one of the more beginner-friendly boat reefs.

Depth
5–35 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
parrotfish, blue tangs, angelfish
Tip: A good pairing with Christ of the Abyss on the same charter since both sit close together and suit beginners.

Tour requiredIntermediate

Molasses Reef

One of the most-dived reefs in the Keys, with a shallow spur-and-groove crest for snorkelers and remnants of the Slobodna wreck below.

Depth
6–35 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
elkhorn coral, brain coral, tropical reef fish
Tip: Stay on the shallow crest with your group; the reef gets busy with dive boats working the deeper sections.
Reef
Tour requiredIntermediate

The Elbow

A reef grown over century-old shipwrecks (including the City of Washington, aground in 1911). The Gulf Stream passes close, so the water is usually clear blue.

Depth
20–35 ft
Getting there
Tour or charter boat access; about 6 miles offshore, marked by a 36-foot light tower.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
elkhorn coral, snapper, porkfish, angelfish, hawksbill turtles, loggerhead turtles
Tip: Expect some current but usually clear water; ledges shelter lots of marine life.
Source: Dive Sites - Pirates Cove Watersports (Key Largo)

Tour requiredIntermediate

French Reef

One of the most spectacular reef areas in the Keys - dozens of ledges, tunnels, and caves large enough to swim through.

Depth
15–30 ft
Getting there
Tour or charter boat access; northeast of Molasses Reef, marked by 'F' buoys.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
tropical reef fish, grouper, moray eels, sponges
Tip: Great for underwater photography; the swim-throughs are the highlight.
Source: Dive Sites - Pirates Cove Watersports (Key Largo)
Reef
Tour requiredBeginner-friendly

Pickles Reef

A large, shallow reef named for Civil War-era pickle barrels sunk here - now covered with the largest pillar corals in the Keys.

Depth
10–25 ft
Getting there
Tour or charter boat access; a large shallow reef close to Molasses Reef.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
pillar coral, sea fans, conch, juvenile tropical fish, lobster
Tip: Also hosts a coral restoration project - look for tagged corals.
Source: 6 Great Places to Go Snorkeling in Key Largo - AFAR

Snapper Ledge

A local favorite named for the countless yellowtail snapper - schools so thick you cannot see through them. Home to one of the largest boulder brain corals in the Upper Keys.

Depth
20–25 ft
Getting there
Tour or charter boat access; a shallow ledge off Key Largo.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
yellowtail snapper, boulder brain coral, tropical reef fish
Tip: Exceptional for both snorkeling and diving; a must-do site in the Keys.
Source: Dive Sites - Pirates Cove Watersports (Key Largo)
