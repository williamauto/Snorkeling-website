Islamorada

A string of named patch reefs and a Sanctuary Preservation Area, almost all boat access.

Boat access only for the named reefs; Founder's Park has limited shore swimming.Free (reefs are open water; boat/tour cost is separate)6 named spots

Best time to go

Calm mornings with light east wind give the clearest water over the patch reefs; afternoon breeze can chop up the crossing.

Getting there

Charters run from marinas along the Overseas Highway; Founder's Park at mile marker 87 has its own free parking lot for the shore option.

Founder's Park

A county park at mile marker 87 with a protected swimming lagoon. Snorkeling here is limited, but it's the only true shore option in Islamorada.

Depth
3–8 ft
Getting there
Walk-in from the park's swimming area.
Fee
Free
Marine life
assorted juvenile fish
Tip: Good for a quick swim or a kids' introduction to the water; not a substitute for the boat-access reefs nearby.

Cheeca Rocks

A shallow patch reef and protected Sanctuary Preservation Area southeast of Upper Matecumbe Key, known for resilient coral heads.

Depth
8–20 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required; it's a NOAA Sanctuary Preservation Area.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
hogfish, green moray eels, grunts, jacks, parrotfish, sea turtles, brain coral, star coral
Tip: Anchoring and touching coral are restricted here since it's a protected sanctuary zone; stick with an operator who knows the mooring rules.

Alligator Reef (Lighthouse)

A reef marked by its 1873 lighthouse, about 4 nautical miles east of Islamorada, and one of the most biodiverse reefs in the Upper Keys.

5–25 ft
Getting there

Fee

Fee

Crocker Reef

A deeper patch reef near Davis Reef, better known for pelagic encounters like eagle rays than shallow coral.

Depth
15–30 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required, near Davis Reef.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
barrel sponges, eagle rays, sea turtles
Tip: Keep an eye on open water rather than just the reef itself; eagle ray sightings often happen mid-water column.
