Key Biscayne

Protected park water, home to Miami's most popular shallow reef plus two offbeat boat trips.

Tour or private-boat access; no walk-in reef.Bill Baggs Cape Florida SP: $8/vehicle5 named spots

Best time to go

Emerald Reef is calmest early on weekday mornings before the mooring field fills with weekend boat traffic.

Getting there

Boats and tours launch from Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park or marinas in Coconut Grove and Key Biscayne.

Shore access: walk right in

No boat or tour needed. Park, walk to the water, and swim out.

Crandon Park Mangrove Reef

A shallow mangrove-fringed reef off Crandon Park's beach - knee-deep water and packed with marine life.

Depth
2–8 ft
Getting there
Wade in from Crandon Park beach; the mangrove reef is rich in marine life.
Fee
Parking fee applies (about $7-10)
Marine life
crabs, barracuda, rays, angelfish
Tip: Miami Eco Adventures runs a kayak-and-snorkel tour from here through Bear Cut Preserve.
Source: Snorkeling in Miami - Miami & Beaches

Bill Baggs Cape Florida

A top-rated state park beach with calm, clear water and easy shore snorkeling, overlooked by the historic Cape Florida lighthouse.

Depth
3–10 ft
Getting there
Wade in from the state park's Atlantic beach at the southern tip of Key Biscayne.
Fee
$8 per vehicle entrance fee
Marine life
tropical reef fish, crabs, rays, angelfish
Tip: The water is typically tranquil thanks to a barrier reef six miles offshore.
Source: Snorkeling in Miami - Miami & Beaches, Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park - Florida State Parks

Mixed access

Reachable more than one way, by tour, private boat, or kayak depending on conditions.

Mixed accessBeginner-friendly

Emerald Reef

A shallow patch reef off Key Biscayne and one of Miami's most popular snorkel destinations thanks to its shallow, calm crest.

Depth
4–15 ft
Getting there
Reachable by tour boat, kayak, or private boat with a mooring permit; there is no walk-in access.
Fee
Bill Baggs Cape Florida SP: $8/vehicle if launching from the park
Marine life
parrotfish, angelfish, sergeant majors, soft coral
Tip: Weekday mornings are far calmer than weekend afternoons, when the mooring field gets crowded with recreational boats.

Boat access

Reachable by private boat or a charter that will run you out and back.

Wreck
Boat accessBeginner-friendly

Half Moon Wreck

A 47-foot yacht that sank in 1930, sitting in shallow enough water for snorkelers as well as divers.

Depth
10–20 ft
Getting there
Boat access only.
Fee
Free
Marine life
puffer fish, spiny lobster, stingrays
Tip: Shallow enough that a calm day gives good visibility on the wreck outline from the surface.

Tour required

Booked through a licensed operator; usually not open to private boats.

Neptune Memorial Reef

An Atlantis-inspired artificial reef and underwater memorial about 3 miles east of Key Biscayne. At 40 feet deep, it suits confident snorkelers best, and most operators pair the trip with a dive.

Depth
35–40 ft
Getting there
Boat or tour required; gear rental is available through Tarpon Lagoon.
Fee
None noted
Marine life
tropical reef fish
Tip: The depth and distance offshore make this better suited to strong swimmers comfortable snorkeling well below the surface.

Book or rent gear

  • Tarpon Lagoon: Neptune Memorial Reef boat trip
